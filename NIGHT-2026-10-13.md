## 1. Judges: evaluator models outside the run

A judge holds no office and changes nothing. Once a month the run gives it the same public record
the delegates see and asks a fixed set of typed questions — will the government survive to the
end (choice, with a probability), how will it end (choice), how severe is this month (score, 0–4
levels), is the evidence enough (noul). Answers land in `log.jsonl` and show in the control room
under the new **Judges** feed chip; the scorecard gains a `judges` block that scores them against
what actually happened: Brier of the survival probabilities, end-class accuracy, severity trend,
the honest "not enough evidence" rate, cost and latency.

The question kinds are TypeSafe's Jev (System One) primitives, so one panel serves two kinds of
backend: any chat model (strict JSON, one repair attempt — what the official adapter does), and
`provider = "systemone"`, which speaks the Jev HTTP contract directly — the hosted API, or any
wire-compatible open server via `base_url`. Contract notes and the open analogs are in
`docs/research/jev-contract.md`; the long-form page is `docs/JUDGES.md`.

Enroll from the Council tab ("Evaluator models observe the run") or in the council file (`judge`,
`judge_backends` — up to three, `judge_every_months`). Verified live: `runs/judge-demo` was judged
by local qwen3.5:0.8b through the room; its verdicts are visible in the feed and its Brier is in
the scorecard. The resolver already survived one real test: a completed run first scored as
collapsed because the natural-end outcome is stamped `"survived"` — fixed and covered.
